# Telegram Channel DOX

## Purpose

- Own Telegram polling/webhook intake, live authorization, media handling, activity, identity mapping, and outbound delivery.

## Local Contracts

- Re-resolve and validate the current allow-list at startup and every inbound/outbound/provider boundary; stale identity mappings or credentials never grant access, and authorization loss terminates useful traffic.
- Persist only minimal identity learned from an authenticated private update, keep webhook secrets and URLs out of status, and permit only teardown `deleteWebhook` after revocation.
- After a successful `getMe`, register the application-supplied command menu best-effort through `setMyCommands` in the `all_private_chats` scope before polling or webhook startup. Reapply global live authorization through the ordinary provider wrapper, never register a group scope, treat provider failure as non-fatal, and propagate authorization loss or caller cancellation; after revocation, retain `deleteWebhook` as the only provider exception.
- Stream bounded media privately, deliver only the final response/error plus validated directives, and keep typing references per route, best-effort, serialized, and bounded. When the default-on live `speech.transcript_echo` is enabled, each successful transcription also echoes once to the same chat or topic as a standalone pre-dispatch message that contains only the shared generated-transcript block and replies to the originating voice or audio message; the echo is not an agent response, and its delivery failure never affects the turn. Translate proactively routed activity through the same refresher and stop it before terminal delivery.
- Build and parse routes only through the strict canonical conversation grammar: `TG-<positive-user-id>`, `TG-<positive-user-id>-topic-<positive-thread-id>`, `TG-group-<negative-chat-id>`, and `TG-group-<negative-chat-id>-topic-<positive-thread-id>`. Non-canonical origins fail closed. Absent threads and the General topic (ID 1) canonicalize to the base conversation and outbound thread ID 0, preserving existing identities; thread IDs of at least 2 select an independent durable conversation and harness session.
- Reapply the current allow-list, verified username mapping, and `group_mode` policy immediately before every route-bound provider call and again before each bounded rate-limit and transient-transport retry. Private topics authorize through their base numeric user, group topics through the current group policy, and thread IDs never grant authorization on their own.
- Keep every outbound family in its originating route: replies, terminal errors, typing actions, welcome messages, transcript echoes, native photo/document attachments, and proactive events carry the route's `message_thread_id` when it addresses a topic, and base or General-topic delivery omits it.
- Deliver replies as independently valid `sendMessage` HTML chunks produced by the markdown chunker: at most 4096 parsed-visible Unicode code points per message and 32768 parsed-visible code points across at most eight messages per reply. Formatting stays balanced, an entity spelling or Unicode code point is never split, grapheme boundaries are preferred while the remaining reply budget allows, and an over-budget reply ends with the deterministic visible truncation marker while complete durable history stays intact. Terminal replies, terminal errors, and direct handler failures render once at enqueue, including any downstream Pi session notice, and persist as private atomic plaintext records under `.spynel/runtime/telegram-replies/` bound to the verified bot account: at most 128 records, 16 per canonical conversation, 512 KiB per record, and 16 MiB per directory, with strict per-route FIFO, at most four concurrent routes, a first-chunk reply anchor, and a persisted acknowledged-chunk cursor. The primary-term worker sends the initial attempt plus five scheduled rounds at 30 seconds, one minute, two minutes, four minutes, and eight minutes measured from each failure's completion, persists the plain-text entity-parse downgrade before its fallback request, honors a usable 429 `retry_after` up to 60 seconds by pushing the next scheduled round later, never adding a request beyond the five scheduled rounds, stops permanently on an unusable or longer 429 without waiting or looping, and reauthorizes the current allow-list, route policy, and original group sender before every stored chunk. Exhausted, permanently refused, and revoked records stay until the one-hour expiry without another send; a confirmed final chunk unlinks the record; capacity or storage refusal never falls back to a direct provider send. The worker runs for the whole primary term even while Telegram is disabled, resumes records after restart, and logs content-free. Transcript echoes, welcome messages, and proactive text stay direct one-shot sends: retry a transient transport failure (timeout, connection reset, closed connection, premature EOF) at most twice after one- and two-second waits; retry one rejected HTML chunk once as plain text only for a provider entity/HTML parse rejection; honor one provider 429 with a usable `retry_after` up to 60 seconds and otherwise fail without waiting or looping; recheck the caller's live context and the exact route authorization before every attempt; and finish one complete direct text delivery, including chunking, retry waits, the rate-limit wait, and the fallback, inside a three-minute delivery budget that caller cancellation or channel shutdown ends. Native attachments stay direct one-shot sends without retries and can arrive before a delayed queued final text. Transcript echoes use `TelegramPlainChunks`, which HTML-escapes literal transcript text without Markdown interpretation so user speech renders exactly as transcribed. Typing, topic naming, attachments, and polling do not retry transient transport failures. A failed direct delivery logs one content-free diagnostic with its category (`deadline`, `transport`, `authorization`, `provider`, or `unknown`) and the executed request count, a direct delivery that recovers after transport retries logs its retry count, queue refusals and stop states log content-free fixed events, and a failed final send never fails the harness job, whose complete response remains in durable job output and history. Because `sendMessage` has no idempotency key, an ambiguous timeout can duplicate one queued or direct chunk, and delivery claims no exactly-once guarantee.
- Carry the authenticated chat/message tuple as private stable source correlation through application acceptance so polling/webhook redelivery cannot duplicate work.
- Rename a private topic at most once per conversation and process. The automatic path (`force=false`) is unconditional on its first trigger, so a newly created thread is never left as "New chat" and the user owns the title after Spynel renames it; every successful rename, including `TOPIC_NOT_MODIFIED`, is marked in a bounded 1024-entry in-memory set with simple eviction, only a conversation already in that set is skipped, and a failed rename is not marked. A restart clears the set and eviction can re-enable one rename beyond 1024 conversations, both accepted edges. No title-state tracking, `forum_topic_created`/`forum_topic_edited` handling, or `is_name_implicit` logic exists.
- `RenameConversation` renames one private topic through `editForumTopic` after parsing the canonical route and rejecting base chats, groups, malformed routes, and invalid labels, and every provider call plus its single bounded retry reapplies live authorization. An automatic repeat for an already-renamed conversation is a silent no-op success, a forced rename from `/pi name` always reaches the provider even after Spynel renamed the topic, a `TOPIC_NOT_MODIFIED` 400 counts as success because it proves the requested title is live, any other provider error propagates unmarked, and a successful or idempotent rename marks the conversation.
